This section develops the mechanism used in the rest of the paper. We do not
attempt to prove full Neural Collapse convergence for deep networks. Existing
work already shows that optimizer choice and weight-decay coupling can change
Neural Collapse behavior \citep{zhao2026optimizer,loshchilov2019decoupled}. Our
goal is different: we turn optimizer update rules into testable predictions about
the penultimate feature geometry that OOD detector families read. The resulting
framework connects three objects: optimization rules, detector-relevant geometry
channels, and diagnostic controls.

\subsection{Setup and claim boundary}
\label{subsec:setup-claim-boundary}

We consider a \(K\)-class classifier decomposed into a penultimate feature map
and a linear classifier,
\begin{equation}
    z(x)=Wh(x)+b,
    \label{eq:classifier-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where \(h(x)\in\mathbb{R}^d\), \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}\), and
\(z(x)\in\mathbb{R}^K\). For ID training features, let \(\mu_c\) denote the
class mean of class \(c\), let each feature be decomposed as
\(h_i=\mu_{y_i}+\varepsilon_i\), and let \(\Sigma_c\) and \(\Sigma_W\) denote the
class-conditional and pooled within-class covariances. The feature residuals
\(\varepsilon_i\), their covariance, the class means, and the classifier weights
are the quantities through which feature-based OOD detectors interact with a
trained model.

We summarize these quantities by a geometry fingerprint
\begin{equation}
    G(h,W)
    =
    \big[
    G_{\mathrm{NC}},
    G_{\mathrm{norm}},
    G_{\mathrm{cov}},
    G_{\mathrm{local}},
    G_{\mathrm{subspace}}
    \big].
    \label{eq:geometry-fingerprint-section3}
\end{equation}
Here \(G_{\mathrm{NC}}\) contains Neural-Collapse-related quantities such as
NC0--NC3 and classifier--feature alignment; \(G_{\mathrm{norm}}\) contains
feature-norm statistics; \(G_{\mathrm{cov}}\) contains within-class dispersion,
covariance spectra, anisotropy, condition number, and effective rank;
\(G_{\mathrm{local}}\) contains local feature-bank neighborhood statistics; and
\(G_{\mathrm{subspace}}\) contains PCA, residual, and class-mean-subspace
structure.

This section provides a mechanistic framework rather than a complete convergence
theory. The analysis uses update-level and locally linearized arguments to
identify which geometry channels should move under different optimizers.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} then tests these predictions across architectures,
optimizers, detectors, and diagnostic controls. Full algebraic details are given
in Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic-derivations}.

\subsection{Weight-decay coupling as a geometry intervention}
\label{subsec:optimizer-update-geometry}

We treat optimizer choice as a training-side intervention on the geometry in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry-fingerprint-section3}. The main optimizer families are
SGD, SGDW, Adam, and AdamW. This set separates two axes that are often
confounded: adaptive preconditioning and weight-decay coupling.

For a fixed total weight decay \(\lambda_{\mathrm{total}}\), define an
interpolation between coupled and decoupled decay:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{\mathrm{coupled}}
    =
    \gamma\lambda_{\mathrm{total}},
    \qquad
    \lambda_{\mathrm{decoupled}}
    =
    (1-\gamma)\lambda_{\mathrm{total}},
    \qquad
    \gamma\in[0,1].
    \label{eq:coupled-decoupled-interpolation-section3}
\end{equation}
The coupled term is included in the gradient update, whereas the decoupled term
is applied as a separate parameter shrinkage. In the Adam family, \(\gamma=0\)
corresponds to AdamW and \(\gamma=1\) corresponds to Adam with coupled L2
regularization.

The key distinction is whether the decay term is seen through the optimizer's
adaptive geometry. With a diagonal adaptive preconditioner \(D_t\), the coupled
Adam update has the approximate form
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{t+1}^{\mathrm{Adam}}
    \approx
    \theta_t-\eta_tD_t\bigl(\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}(\theta_t)+\lambda\theta_t\bigr),
    \label{eq:adam-coupled-preconditioned-section3}
\end{equation}
whereas the decoupled AdamW update has the approximate form
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{t+1}^{\mathrm{AdamW}}
    \approx
    (1-\eta_t\lambda)\theta_t-\eta_tD_t\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}(\theta_t).
    \label{eq:adamw-decoupled-preconditioned-section3}
\end{equation}
Thus Adam applies weight decay in the preconditioned geometry, while AdamW
applies weight decay in raw parameter space. This difference can change
direction-dependent contraction in the penultimate representation, affecting
within-class dispersion, covariance eigenspectra, feature-norm statistics, and
Neural-Collapse-related alignment even when ID accuracy is similar. The
AdamW-to-Adam interpolation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coupled-decoupled-interpolation-section3}
therefore provides a controlled path for testing whether geometry and detector
behavior move along the weight-decay-coupling axis.

\subsection{Detector families as geometry readouts}
\label{subsec:geometry-channels-detectors}

Feature-based OOD detectors read different channels of the geometry in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry-fingerprint-section3}. Distance-based detectors read class
centers and radial scale; density-based detectors read covariance and volume;
neighborhood detectors read local feature-bank spacing; angular detectors read
prototype or classifier directions; and subspace detectors read principal or

\subsection{Diagnostic controls}
\label{subsec:diagnostic-recipe}

The preceding readout view suggests that detector failures should be diagnosed
by geometry channel, not by detector name alone. We therefore use three families
of diagnostic controls.

First, L2 normalization removes the radial feature-norm channel. If raw
Mahalanobis or raw kNN degrades under one optimizer but recovers after L2
normalization, the gap is likely carried by radial or norm-coupled geometry.

Second, covariance controls probe estimation and conditioning. Tied, diagonal,
and shrinkage covariance variants test whether Gaussian feature-density or
Mahalanobis-style failures arise from unstable covariance estimation,
ill-conditioning, anisotropy, or broader representation mismatch.

Third, PCA and residual-subspace controls test whether detector gaps are carried
by principal ID directions or residual directions. Projected features isolate the
dominant ID subspace, while residual scores test whether samples occupy
directions not captured by the ID class structure.

These controls are not proposed as new OOD detectors. They are diagnostic
interventions used to localize the geometry channel responsible for a detector
gap. Formal definitions are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic-interventions}.

\subsection{Testable predictions}
\label{subsec:testable-predictions}

The framework yields six testable predictions for Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Prediction 1: weight-decay coupling should move geometry.}
In an AdamW-to-Adam interpolation, ID accuracy may remain comparable while
NC-related metrics, feature-norm statistics, covariance spectra, and detector
behavior move along the coupling axis.

\paragraph{Prediction 2: adaptive coupling gaps should be larger than non-adaptive gaps.}
Because coupled and decoupled decay are much closer under non-adaptive SGD than
under adaptive preconditioning, the SGD--SGDW geometry gap should generally be
smaller than the Adam--AdamW gap under comparable training conditions.

\paragraph{Prediction 3: radial geometry should explain raw distance failures.}
When optimizer choice increases feature-norm dispersion or class-dependent norm
variation, raw Mahalanobis and raw kNN should degrade more than their
L2-normalized counterparts. L2 recovery should be strongest in regimes where the
raw gap is radial or norm-coupled.

\paragraph{Prediction 4: covariance geometry should explain density failures.}
When optimizer choice changes covariance spectra, anisotropy, condition number,
or effective rank, Mahalanobis and DDU-style Gaussian density scores should
change accordingly. Shrinkage, diagonalization, and PCA controls should reveal
whether the failure is due to covariance estimation or a broader geometry
mismatch.

\paragraph{Prediction 5: subspace and prototype geometry should explain angular and NC-based detectors.}
When class-mean geometry and classifier alignment are stable, angular and
collapse-based readouts should remain compatible. When effective rank or
residual energy changes, PCA-based and NECO-style residual diagnostics should be
more sensitive.

\paragraph{Prediction 6: ID geometry should inform detector diagnostics.}
If the geometry fingerprint \(G(h,W)\) captures detector-relevant structure, then
ID-only geometry measurements should help recommend which detector family or
diagnostic control is likely to be compatible before using OOD validation data.
